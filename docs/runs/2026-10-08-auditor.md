## Why the shuffled control failed: a diagnosis, not a reading

**The control was flawed by design.** Labels were flipped per pair at random, about half the pairs.
Per seed, the shuffled heads swing to both sides of chance and follow which direction is the
majority:

| Seed | Pairs flipped (of 603) | Majority | auditor-mean: qwen / qwen3b / llama |
|---|---|---|---|
| 42 | 286 | true | 0.45 / 0.75 / 0.80 |
| 43 | 321 | flipped | 0.33 / 0.21 / 0.26 |
| 44 | 303 | about even | 0.48 / 0.24 / 0.71 |

- The planted edit is so easy to learn that a head finds it even under random labels, and takes its
  sign from the label majority.
- A leak through length, position or formatting would push every seed the same way. This doesn't.
- A length-only rule ("the longer answer is flawed") scores 0.587 on validation, 0.539 on the judge
  set and 0.511 on the second-planter set.
- So the control can't tell "a leak" from "a learnable edit with a random sign".

**Not adopted:** re-reading the shuffled control symmetrically (|accuracy − 0.5| per seed, with a
sign test). That would be a rule chosen after the role numbers were known. It is noted here as
exploratory only, with no reading attached.

**The proposed replacement is a "Skeptic" control.**
- Plant one-sentence paraphrase edits that add no error, on prompts no head has seen, and check
  whether the Auditors fire on them.
- Its rule can be committed before its data exists.
- It asks the question the pointwise AUCs below raise: are the Auditors finding *errors*, or only
  *which version was edited*?
- It needs the maintainer's go.

Advocate-span judges a located change (it pools over the edited span, which exists only when both
answers are shown), so it is not compared like-for-like with the Auditor's single-answer task.

**What stands out:**
- **The heads separate a pair, but don't flag a lone answer.**
  - Every mean-pooled Auditor reaches 0.89–0.97 pairwise, but its pointwise AUC (flawed against
    strong answers across prompts) is only 0.53–0.57.
  - The found Auditor is the same: 0.678 pairwise and AUC 0.506.
  - They tell a pair's two versions apart, but don't reliably flag a lone flawed answer. That is
    consistent with detecting "which version was edited" rather than "which one is wrong", and the
    Skeptic control would test it.
  - Advocate-span's AUC of 0.79–0.86 is the exception.
- **The Advocate-mean decisions are mostly near-ties on Qwen features.** Their noise-band accuracy
  falls to about 0.50 on qwen and qwen3b, so their pairwise accuracy rests on gaps within the
  measured scoring noise.
- **Llama features did best**, near Kev-4B's 0.976 on the judge set with auditor-mean.
- **No sign of Qwen recognising its own family's edits.**
  - On every form and set, the size-matched comparison has Llama features level with or ahead of
    Qwen2.5-3B, including on the Qwen-planted judge set.
  - The family contrast (the difference of differences, qwen3b vs llama) is **inconclusive** for
    every form; for example, auditor-mean gives 0.119 [−0.004, 0.263].
- **Not one critic counted twice.** The Auditor and Advocate correlate at Pearson −0.77 / +0.76 /
  −0.53 across the three sources, with error consistency 0.44 / 0.17 / 0.72 and double faults of 3–4%.
- **The mid-layer diagnostic (exploratory):** auditor-mid is close to auditor-mean on every source
  (0.92–0.96 on validation), so depth isn't the bottleneck.
- **Head sizes:** 1.45 M (qwen), 1.71 M (qwen3b), 2.23 M (llama) parameters.

**The second-planter set** (47 gemma4:31b pairs on 24 unused prompts):
- Gemma's edits are larger than Qwen's: a median of 8 characters against 5, under the plan's 2×
  caveat line. They also keep the answer's length unchanged less often (17% against 36%).
- Most heads' transfer readings came out "transfers", but no transfer reading is made, since no
  role reading is.
