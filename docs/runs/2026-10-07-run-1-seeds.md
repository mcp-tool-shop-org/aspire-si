# Run report: run 1 of the next-runs plan, seeds 43 and 44 (2026-10-07)

The [fine-tune-then-ASPIRE experiment](2026-10-07-sft-then-aspire.md) reported a headline
result on one training run per condition: with the composite teacher, the critic trained after
the fine-tune spotted planted errors less often than the control's, 0.646 against 0.866. Run 1 of
the [next-runs plan](2026-10-07-next-runs-plan.md) trained two more seeds of every condition to
find out whether that holds.

**It does not.** Over three seeds, neither teacher shows a reliable difference. The critic's
accuracy varies much more between training runs than between conditions. The hidden-state
trajectory, though, replicates closely.

## Setup

Each seed repeats the seed-42 experiment at a new seed: four critics trained exactly as on
2026-10-07, from the same data.

| Condition | Student | Teacher |
|---|---|---|
| control, local | base | Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct |
| control, composite | base | Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct + Gemma-4-31B-it, voting |
| fine-tune + ASPIRE, local | that seed's fine-tune | Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct |
| fine-tune + ASPIRE, composite | that seed's fine-tune | Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct + Gemma-4-31B-it, voting |

- A new seed changes the LoRA and critic initialisation, the fine-tune's shuffling and the
  sampled dialogues, so it also changes the teacher scores the critic learns from. Each run had
  its own output folder, so no dialogue cache was shared.
- Every critic was measured on the same 127 judge pairs: a strong answer, and the same answer
  with one planted error. The 95% intervals resample prompts.
- Code: `examples/sft-experiment/pod/plan_c.sh SEED`, with configs written by `seed_configs.py`.
- Hardware: one RTX PRO 6000 (96 GB) per seed. The two composite runs trained side by side
  (77.8 of 96 GB), then the two local runs.

## The rule (written before the runs)

The bar was set per teacher, over three seeds counting seed 42:

| Result | Reading |
|---|---|
| All three fine-tune critics below all three control critics, and a mean drop of at least 0.10 | The fine-tune hurts the critic |
| A mean drop of at least 0.10, but the seed ranges overlap | A tendency |
| A mean drop under 0.10 | No reliable difference; the seed-42 drop was mostly one draw, and the report and CHANGELOG are corrected |

## Results

Pairwise accuracy on all 127 pairs: the fraction of pairs where the critic scores the strong
answer higher, with ties counted as half.

| Critic | Seed 42 | Seed 43 | Seed 44 | Mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| control, composite | 0.866 | 0.803 | 0.425 [0.333, 0.516] | 0.698 |
| fine-tune + ASPIRE, composite | 0.646 | 0.504 | 0.764 [0.675, 0.844] | 0.638 |
| control, local | 0.724 | 0.543 | 0.638 | 0.635 |
| fine-tune + ASPIRE, local | 0.638 | 0.732 | 0.512 | 0.627 |

**Reading under the rule: no reliable difference, for either teacher.**

| Teacher | Mean drop | Seed ranges |
|---|---|---|
| Composite | 0.06 | Overlap; seed 44 reverses the order, with its fine-tuned critic the best of the four |
| Local | 0.008 | Overlap; seeds 42 and 44 point one way, seed 43 the other |

The 31-pair teacher-detectable subset shows the same pattern and decides nothing either.

**Critic accuracy varies far more between training runs than between conditions.**
- The composite control critic ranged from 0.425 to 0.866 across seeds. Seed 44's interval is
  [0.333, 0.516], so that critic does no better than chance, and may do worse.
- The local control ranged from 0.543 to 0.724.

With 32 training prompts and three epochs, which critic a run produces is largely a matter of the
draw. A comparison of two single runs, like the seed-42 result, says little.

**Training loss does not predict this accuracy.** The critic loss fell in most runs. Seed 44's
best critic (fine-tune + ASPIRE, composite, 0.764) is one whose loss rose, from 0.91 to 1.10.
Fitting the teacher's scores on 32 prompts and separating planted errors on unseen prompts are
different abilities.

**Checks.**
- All 12 runs trained on 32 dialogues, and the dialogues differ between seeds.
- The teachers' mean scores during training were stable across seeds: local 6.8–7.2, composite
  5.1–5.5.
- Nothing points to a fault in any run.

## The hidden-state trajectory replicates

The probes ran as before: the control's 32 fixed exchanges go through each model, and each
exchange's state is compared with its own state in the base or the fine-tuned student.

| | Local teacher | Composite teacher |
|---|---|---|
| Fine-tune drift size, seeds 42 / 43 / 44 | 20.7 / 22.8 / 21.4 | 20.2 / 22.2 / 20.9 |
| Fine-tune drift direction, cosine between seeds | 0.98 for every pair | 0.98 for every pair |
| ASPIRE after the fine-tune against the fine-tune's drift (cosine) | −0.77 / −0.49 / −0.72 | −0.77 / −0.73 / −0.56 |
| Control ASPIRE (no fine-tune) against the fine-tune's drift, seeds 43 / 44 | −0.75 / −0.73 | −0.57 / −0.71 |
| Control ASPIRE against ASPIRE after the fine-tune, seeds 43 / 44 | +0.68 / +0.73 | +0.67 / +0.81 |

- **The fine-tune moves the student's representations the same way every time.** The direction
  barely changes between seeds.
- **ASPIRE has a direction of its own that opposes it.** This holds in every seed and with both
  teachers, whether or not the student was fine-tuned first. Without a fine-tune, ASPIRE still
  moves the student against the direction the fine-tune would have taken it.
- As before, none of these drifts tracks the teacher's quality scores beyond a random-direction
  null.

This is the experiment's finding that holds:
- ASPIRE's representation training and supervised fine-tuning on teacher answers pull a student's
  hidden states in opposing directions.
- The critic's accuracy is not robustly helped or hurt by which one comes first.

## What this changes

- The 2026-10-07 report and the CHANGELOG said the fine-tune made the composite critic worse.
  They are corrected in this change: over three seeds there is no reliable difference.
- A meaningful comparison of ASPIRE critics needs several seeds per condition and more training
  prompts. At 32 prompts, the run-to-run spread (up to 0.44) swamps any plausible effect.

## Next options (none approved yet)

- **More training prompts, or more seeds,** so that critic variance stops dominating. Measuring
  how the spread shrinks with the prompt count would itself be useful.
- **A stronger fine-tune** (more epochs or a higher learning rate), re-checked against the
  held-out answer bar of run 2 before any ASPIRE runs on it.
- **A forced-choice judge as a reference.** For example, an open one-pass decision model, measured
  on the same 127 pairs in both orders. It would put the critics and the 32B teacher's position
  bias in context.
- **#11, the policy-gradient term,** so that the student is trained toward better answers.
